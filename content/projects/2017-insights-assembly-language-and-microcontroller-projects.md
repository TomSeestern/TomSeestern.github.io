Between 2016 and 2019, I worked on school projects with AT89C5131A microcontrollers and Assembly. We built two separate programs: one controlled a Gleichstrommotor with pulse-width modulation, another sent dynamic text to an LCD through serial communication.

Assembly was unforgiving compared with C++ or SQF. A small mistake could mean nothing happened, and understanding microcontroller behaviour meant working through code, wiring, and hardware setup together. I did not get it immediately. Debugging forced me to slow down and account for what every instruction and connection did.

I worked with classmates, sharing observations when motor control or LCD output did not behave as expected. We planned, wrote and debugged Assembly, assembled hardware, then tested real setup rather than stopping at code that looked plausible.

These projects gave me clearer view of boundary between software and electronics. Controlling a motor and LCD through AT89C5131A made low-level code feel concrete, and strengthened my interest in robotics.
