As an IT consultant, I worked with municipal departments that had valuable information spread across existing software, historical records, and disconnected reporting processes. The goal was practical: give staff a dependable view of their data without forcing a disruptive replacement of every system.

I designed a cloud data-warehousing solution around the existing application landscape. Azure provided the cloud foundation, while Office 365 and Power BI made the reporting layer accessible in tools already familiar to staff. The work included integrating source systems, digitizing and migrating historical data, and building the structures needed to keep reporting consistent across departments.

I created Power BI reports for teams including HR and event management, focused on metrics that supported day-to-day decisions. Rather than deliver one generic dashboard, I worked with department heads and IT staff to refine reports through feedback and user testing. That collaboration mattered because self-service reporting only works when people can understand and trust the data behind it.

The project moved through needs assessment, architecture and migration planning, implementation, and end-user training. Its lasting value was not only the data warehouse. It established a repeatable way for the administration to turn operational data into usable information.

Looking back, I would now define data ownership, automated quality checks, and refresh monitoring more explicitly from the start. This project gave me an early foundation in cloud data architecture and showed how technical choices must fit the people and processes around them.
