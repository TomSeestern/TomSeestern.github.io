From 2014 to 2016, I worked with a small group of friends on Altis Life, an Arma 3 roleplay gamemode. I was the lead developer and project manager. We used SQF, SQL, Windows Server 2012, Arma 3, and TeamSpeak 3 to turn a military simulation into a player-run world.

The core idea was a gamified version of real life. Players chose a side, civilian or police, then picked a legal or illegal job, earned money, bought vehicles, and built and customised their own house. Each player created a character and stayed in role during interactions. Later versions added stock markets, more factions, additional jobs, clothing, skins, and unique events.

The first challenge was scale. More players meant more server pressure, more edge cases in custom mechanics, and more community work. We kept adding features, watching performance, collecting feedback, and updating the gamemode instead of treating launch as the finish line. A feature that worked with twenty players could fail with two hundred, and the only reliable way to find out was to test it under load.

I led a small team of developers and moderators, oversaw the servers, and handled project management. That meant splitting work, reviewing changes, and deciding when a feature was stable enough to ship. It also meant communicating with players when something broke, because a roleplay server is a social product as much as a technical one.

## What I took from it

Altis Life reached more than 300 concurrent players on our community servers, and affiliated servers adopted the project. That number was exciting, but it also made every bad decision visible. I learned to balance new ideas with stability and to treat the player community as part of the project, not an audience sitting outside it.

It was the first project where I had to lead both technical work and people. It taught me that roleplay systems only work when code, server performance, and clear shared rules hold together.
