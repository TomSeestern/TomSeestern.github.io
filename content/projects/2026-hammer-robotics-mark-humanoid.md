Hammer Robotics is a Munich-based company building robots for work that is difficult to staff and physically demanding. I founded the company in 2024 and lead it as CEO. Our first product, Mark, is an AI-driven humanoid robot aimed at high-load logistics automation, starting with baggage handling at airports.

The problem is concrete. Baggage handling has a chronic labour shortage, and the work is repetitive, physically punishing, and safety-critical. An airport cannot solve that with another dashboard. It needs a machine that can perceive a cluttered environment, decide what to grasp, and move a heavy bag without creating a new risk for the people working nearby.

We built a fully functional prototype for autonomous baggage unloading within six months. The team was three engineers, which forced us to keep the architecture focused: perception, planning, and control had to be testable independently, and the hardware had to be robust enough to survive repeated iteration. We used simulation to shorten the loop between a software change and a physical test, then validated the behaviour on real hardware.

That work led to pilot preparation with the three largest airports in Germany. It also helped us secure €300k in non-dilutive grants and €25k in awards, and attracted three term sheets from top-tier VCs and industry experts. Those signals matter, but the engineering milestone matters more: a robot that can do a useful piece of the job in a real environment.

## What I took from it

Hammer Robotics is a homecoming to the robotics work I started as a student. It combines the parts of my background that fit together best: AI models, real-time systems, hardware constraints, and a product problem that is easy to explain and hard to solve.

The next phase is about turning a prototype into a dependable system: broader field validation, safety cases, maintainability, and a team that can support customers after the demo. That is the part I find most interesting. A humanoid robot is only impressive if it keeps working when the room is not set up for a video.
