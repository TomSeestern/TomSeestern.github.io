At Technisches Gymnasium, I spent 2016 to 2019 moving from C++ exercises into a time-tracking application. Before that final project, I built Snake and Sinking Ships clones. Small games gave me a safe place to make mistakes before I had to manage real user entries.

Time tracker used C++, Qt Creator, SQLite, and CSV export. I wanted employees to record and edit working time, but turning that into useful desktop interface was harder than writing another game loop. GUI decisions and reliable persistence both kept exposing gaps in my understanding.

I worked with fellow students, compared solutions, and kept refining app. SQLite gave entries a place to live between sessions, while CSV export made data portable. Neither came for free. I had to connect UI actions, storage, and error cases into something that did not fall apart after restart.

Project showed me why I like building tools. It took classroom C++ into something practical and taught me that useful software depends on details users never see until they fail.
