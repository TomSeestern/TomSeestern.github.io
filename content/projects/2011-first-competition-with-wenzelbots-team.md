In 2011, I joined Wenzelbots for my first national robotics competition. We built and programmed a LEGO Mindstorms NXT robot in LabVIEW, then had to make it complete several tasks in one run. That sounded manageable until we began testing.

We were younger and less experienced than many teams around us. Scores made this obvious. Our robot needed repeated adjustments, and every change in design or program meant another test. Still, process mattered as much as result: brainstorm, prototype, program, test, then prepare presentation.

We did not finish near top of leaderboard, but competition gave me first real experience with planning work as team. I learned that a working technical idea is not enough. Someone must organise tests, explain research, and keep group moving when run fails.

That year made robotics feel bigger than a school club. It was first time I saw programming, hardware, presentation, and teamwork collide under deadline.
