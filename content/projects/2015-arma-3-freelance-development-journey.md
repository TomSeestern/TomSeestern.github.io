Altis Life grew from hobby modding into freelance work between 2014 and 2016. I led development and project management while still balancing school. The core stack was SQF, with C++ and Python also part of the work.

We extended roleplay servers with stock markets, additional factions, and custom events. Each feature had to fit an economy and social system that players were already using. Adding a stock market was not only a scripting task; it changed how players earned money, how factions competed, and how much support the moderators needed. Server stability was a constant concern once community servers reached more than 300 concurrent players.

Growth brought affiliate servers around the world and freelance requests from people running their own communities. I learned quickly that adding a feature is the easy part. Supporting it, debugging it under load, and explaining it to people who run their own servers takes far more work. A change that looked harmless in a test could break an economy or create an exploit in production.

Freelance work also taught me to scope a request before writing code. Clients often described a symptom rather than the underlying system. I had to ask what they wanted players to experience, then decide whether the fix belonged in a script, a database, or the server configuration.

## What I took from it

This period gave me my first experience of being responsible for software that people depended on for their community. It made me more deliberate about testing changes, handling feedback, and finishing work instead of chasing the next idea.
