I started making Arma 3 multiplayer missions in early 2013. Most were for small groups of four to eight players. I used Arma 3 Mission Editor and SQF to build objectives around hostage rescues, reconnaissance, and aerial combat.

SQF felt familiar in places because I already knew some C++ and Python, but it also had limits and debugging quirks that took time to accept. First mission files were small and messy. I would test them with players, find failures in real matches, then change scripts and try again.

Community feedback shaped most decisions. A mission that read well on paper could become boring or confusing once people played it together. That pushed me to think about objectives, pacing, and failure states, not only code.

Those missions made programming social for me. I was not writing exercises anymore. I was making shared experiences and learning from people using them.
