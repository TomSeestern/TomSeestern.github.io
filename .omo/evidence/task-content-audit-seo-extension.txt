## Research basis

Sources read:
- https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies
- https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content
- https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2023/02/google-search-and-ai-content
- https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing

Google policy used:
- Scaled content abuse: many pages generated primarily to manipulate rankings,
  including generative-AI pages without added value.
- Keyword stuffing: repeating words or phrases so often that it sounds unnatural.
- Doorway abuse: substantially similar pages created for similar queries.
- Scraped/duplicate content and thin affiliate pages are low-value patterns.
- Google's AI guidance: "Appropriate use of AI or automation is not against our
  guidelines"; using it primarily to manipulate rankings violates spam policies.
- Helpful-content guidance: original information, descriptive non-exaggerated
  titles, first-hand experience, and people-first rather than search-engine-first
  content.

Wikipedia AI-writing sections used:
- Vague expression of connection or association
- Vague attributions and overgeneralization of opinions
- Outline-like conclusions about challenges and future prospects
- Promotional and advertisement-like language
- Avoidance of basic copulatives
- Title case and boldface (considered, not added as standalone rules because of
  house style and functional recipe labels)
- Collaborative communication and hedging disclaimers
- Phrasal templates
- Ineffective indicators: isolated transitions, perfect grammar, and formal prose
  are not reliable tells; em-dash overuse is historical.

## Implemented rules

AI-writing tells:
- vague-attribution
- hedging-disclaimer
- formulaic-phrase
- outline-conclusion
- promotional-language

Search-quality / spam anti-patterns:
- keyword-stuffing: three content words, at least five occurrences, at least 2% of
  document words
- duplicate-content: exact 12-word shingle shared by two Markdown content files
