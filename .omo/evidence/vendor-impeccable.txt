CORRECTION — the nice restore never worked
=========================================
`renice -n 19` (lowering priority) is permitted for the same user; putting a
process back to a lower nice value (5 or 0) is NOT, without CAP_SYS_NICE.
Captured proof:
  $ renice -n 5 -p 3013335
  renice: failed to set priority for 3013335 (process ID): Permission denied
The restore loop hid this behind `2>/dev/null || true`, so "RESTORED 1816
process(es)" was a false statement: those calls all failed. Effect: processes
alive during the demotion windows stayed at nice 19 (children inherited 19 too).
Live residue right now: 10 stryker workers, 2 JVMs, 1 chromium, 1 bash —
  2144838 2417842 3013335 3014220 3294943 3294990 3294992 3295010 3481920
  3488427 3517779 3519149 3535203 3536022
Fix needs root (`sudo renice -n 5 -p <pids>`) or their natural exit; new
processes spawn at the default nice.
Lesson: never demote another workload unless the restore path is privileged and
verified; and never report a restore that was not observed to succeed.
